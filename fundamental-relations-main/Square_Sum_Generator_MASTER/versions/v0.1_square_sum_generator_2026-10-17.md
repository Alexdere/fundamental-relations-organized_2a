## 1. Frames and definitions

- **F-G (integer Euclidean geometry):** the lengths and diagonal of right triangles; squared lengths add by Pythagoras.
- **F-T (triangular-number arithmetic):** `T_j = j(j+1)/2` for a nonnegative integer `j`.
- **F-P (prime arithmetic):** `p_k` is the k-th positive prime, starting `p_1 = 2`; `S_k = sum_{i=1}^k p_i²`.
- **F-C (computational finite-domain evidence):** exhaustive checks over specified finite ranges; not universal proofs.
- **F-H (hypothesis):** speculative relations not derived by a proof.

A statement at one frame is not automatically a statement at another. Particularly, a radical can be a valid Euclidean length without being an integer.

## 2. Infinite identity (PROVED, F-T and F-G)

For every integer `n >= 1`:

`T_{n-1}² + T_n² = T_{n²} = n²(n²+1)/2`.

**Proof.** Since `T_{n-1}=n(n−1)/2` and `T_n=n(n+1)/2`, the sum of their squares is

`n²[(n−1)²+(n+1)²]/4 = n²[2(n²+1)]/4 = n²(n²+1)/2 = T_{n²}`.

Examples:

| n | First leg | Second leg | Sum of squares | Integer hypotenuse? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 5 | 10 | 15 | 325 | No |
| 6 | 15 | 21 | 666 | No |
| 7 | 21 | 28 | 1225 | Yes: 35 |
| 41 | 820 | 861 | 1413721 | Yes: 1189 |

**Reverse characterization (PROVED, F-T):** If nonnegative integers `a,b` satisfy `a²+b²=T_{a+b}`, then `(a−b)²=a+b`. Thus `a+b=n²`, `a−b=±n` and `{a,b}={T_{n−1},T_n}` for some integer `n>=0` (with `n=0` giving `a=b=0`). There are no other pairs.

## 3. Integral diagonal is a negative Pell equation (PROVED, F-G/F-T)

For `a=T_{n−1}`, `b=T_n`, the hypotenuse `d` is integer if and only if

`n² − 2m² = −1` for some integer `m>=1`, with `d=n*m`.

**Proof.** `d²=n²(n²+1)/2`. Since `n² | d²`, any integer `d` is a multiple of `n` (prime valuations). Write `d=nm`; then `m²=(n²+1)/2` and `n²−2m²=−1`. Conversely such a pair gives integral `d=nm`.

All positive solutions occur by taking odd powers of `1+√2`:

`n_j + m_j√2 = (1+√2)^(2j+1)`, `j>=0`.

Equivalently, start `(n,m)=(1,1)` and iterate `(n,m) -> (3n+4m, 2n+3m)`. First nonzero-angle examples:

`(n,m)=(7,5),(41,29),(239,169),(1393,985),...`

Their right triangles start `(21,28,35)` and `(820,861,1189)`.

## 4. Twin-prime generator and mutually exclusive closure (PROVED, F-P/F-G)

The triangular legs are

`a = (n/2)(n−1)`, `b=(n/2)(n+1)`.

If `n−1` and `n+1` are both odd primes (a twin-prime pair), then `n` must be even, and for every even `n` the resulting diagonal **cannot** be an integer: `n²+1` is odd, so `(n²+1)/2` is not an integer square. Equivalently, negative Pell solutions have odd `n`. Thus the twin-prime-labeled family and integral-diagonal Pell family **cannot intersect under this construction**.

For twin prime pairs greater than `(3,5)`, the center `n` is divisible by 6. In particular `n=6` is the first such center (`5,7`) and yields `a=15`, `b=21`, and `a²+b²=666`.

**Caution:** This theorem excludes integer hypotenuses only *inside the particular triangular-leg generator*. It does not rule out other right triangles, other prime relations, or perfect cuboids in general.

## 5. First-seven-prime-square collision (EXACT IDENTITY + EMPIRICAL SCAN, F-P/F-C)

`2² + 3² + 5² + 7² + 11² + 13² + 17² = 666`.

This coincides with `T_{6²}=666` and with `T_5² + T_6² = 666`.

The identity `S_k = T_{n²}` is equivalently the quartic Diophantine condition

`8*S_k + 1 = (2n²+1)²`.

At `(k,n)=(7,6)`, `8*666+1=5329=73²`, with `73=2*6²+1`.

**Computational check, not a proof of uniqueness:** Scanned all prime prefixes with last prime `< 10,000,000` (664,579 primes), calculating every `S_k` with exact integers. Only two prefixes were triangular:

- `k=7`, last prime `17`: `S_7=666=T_36=T_{6²}`.
- `k=86`, last prime `443`: `S_86=5,022,865=T_3169` (`3169` is *not* a square).

Thus only `(k,n)=(7,6)` satisfied `S_k=T_{n²}` in this finite search. This does **not** establish that no later solutions exist, nor that the meeting at 666 is non-random.

## 6. What survives / what remains open

**Proved:**
1. The exact, infinite consecutive-triangular square-sum identity.
2. Complete reverse characterization of pairs solving `a²+b²=T_{a+b}`.
3. Pell characterization and infinite integer-hypotenuse subfamily.
4. Parity-disjointness between the twin-prime-centered subfamily and the Pell subfamily.

**Empirically checked:** The `666` prime-prefix / square-index-triangular intersection is the only one for primes below 10 million.

**Open:**
- Whether `S_k=T_{n²}` has any further solutions for other k,n.
- Whether a deeper generator relates the ordering of initial primes to triangular legs beyond this one numerical equality.
- Whether a meaningful null model gives statistical significance to the first match.
- Whether further polygonal-number constructions yield useful Pell families or new obstructions.

**Method:** Generate candidates liberally, prove valid identities, distinguish frames, check counterexamples and null models, and preserve all versions without retroactively laundering conjectures into theorems.

## 7. Reproduction code

```python
from sympy import primerange
from math import isqrt

S = 0
triangular_prefixes = []
square_index_prefixes = []
for k, p in enumerate(primerange(2, 10_000_000), 1):
    S += p*p
    root = isqrt(8*S+1)
    if root*root == 8*S+1:
        t = (root-1)//2
        triangular_prefixes.append((k, p, t, S))
        n = isqrt(t)
        if n*n == t:
            square_index_prefixes.append((k, p, n, S))

print(triangular_prefixes)
print(square_index_prefixes)
# [(7, 17, 36, 666), (86, 443, 3169, 5022865)]
# [(7, 17, 6, 666)]
```
