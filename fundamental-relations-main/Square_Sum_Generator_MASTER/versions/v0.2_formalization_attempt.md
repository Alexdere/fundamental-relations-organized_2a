## 0. Frames and logical typing

- **F-T — elementary arithmetic:** integers, squares, triangular numbers.
- **F-G — Euclidean geometry:** lengths of right-triangle legs and diagonal, including irrational lengths.
- **F-D — Diophantine geometry:** F-G with integer restrictions on the diagonal.
- **F-P — ordered prime-prefix arithmetic:** primes in ascending order; sums of their squares.
- **F-C — finite computational test:** observations within explicitly bounded input sets.
- **F-H — conjectural mechanism:** proposed explanatory links or generative correspondence.
- **F-R — Fundamental Relations interpretation:** conjectural account of restrictions, frames, closure, and interactions.

A statement must not move between frames without an explicit translation and proof of any newly added restriction.

## 1. Basic generator

**Definition 1.1 (triangular number; F-T).** For each integer (j\ge0), let (T_j=j(j+1)/2).

**Definition 1.2 (square-sum map; F-T).** For an integer (n\ge1), define
$$
a_n=T_{n-1}=n(n-1)/2,\quad b_n=T_n=n(n+1)/2,quad H_n=a_n^2+b_n^2.
$$
Here (a_n,b_n) are nonnegative integers and (H_n) is a nonnegative integer.

**Theorem 1.3 (exact identity; PROVED, F-T).**
$$
H_n=T_{n^2}=\frac{n^2(n^2+1)}2.
$$
**Proof.** Expand the definition:
$$
H_n=\frac{n^2}{4}[(n-1)^2+(n+1)^2]
=\frac{n^2}{4}(2n^2+2)=\frac{n^2(n^2+1)}2.
$$
By the triangular-number definition this is (T_{n^2}). QED.

**Theorem 1.4 (converse / complete classification; PROVED, F-T).** For nonnegative integers (a,b),
$$
a^2+b^2=T_{a+b}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\{a,b\}=\{T_{n-1},T_n\}
$$
for some integer (n\ge1), or (a=b=0) for the degenerate (n=0) case.

**Proof.** Put (s=a+b) and (r=a-b). The identity (2(a^2+b^2)=s^2+r^2) and (2T_s=s(s+1)) imply (r^2=s). Thus (s=n^2), (r=\pm n), with (n\ge0); consequently (a=(n^2\pm n)/2), (b=(n^2\mp n)/2). These are consecutive triangular numbers. Reverse substitution proves sufficiency. QED.

**Interpretation (F-R, PROPOSAL).** This is an exact closure condition under a *chosen* composition rule: taking the squared norm of consecutive triangular numbers returns another triangular number. It is not yet a universal claim about physical self-reference.

## 2. Integer geometric closure

**Definition 2.1 (diagonal; F-G).** Let (d_n=\sqrt{H_n}\), the nonnegative Euclidean hypotenuse of the right triangle with legs (a_n,b_n).

**Theorem 2.2 (Pell criterion; PROVED, F-D).** The diagonal (d_n) is an integer iff there is a positive integer (m) such that
$$
n^2-2m^2=-1,
\\qquad d_n=nm.
$$
**Proof.** First suppose \(n\) is even; write \(n=2t\). Then
\[
d_n^2=2t^2(4t^2+1).
\]
Because \(4t^2+1\) is odd, the exponent of 2 in the prime factorization of the right-hand side is \(1+2v_2(t)\), an odd number, whereas the square of an integer has even prime exponents. So **no even \(n\) permits an integer diagonal**.

Now \(n\) is odd, making \((n^2+1)/2\) an integer. Therefore
\[
d_n^2=n^2\big((n^2+1)/2\big).
\]
If \(d_n\) is an integer, prime valuations imply \(n\mid d_n\). Write \(d_n=nm\); cancelling \(n^2\) gives \(m^2=(n^2+1)/2\), equivalent to \(n^2-2m^2=-1\). Conversely every positive integer Pell solution produces an integer diagonal \(d_n=nm\). QED.

**Corollary 2.3 (infinite solutions; PROVED, F-D).** All positive solutions arise as
$$
n_j+m_j\sqrt2=(1+\sqrt2)^{2j+1},\quad j\ge0.
$$
The recurrence is
$$
(n,m)\mapsto(3n+4m,,2n+3m).
$$
Starting with ((1,1)) yields ((7,5),(41,29),(239,169),\ldots). The first gives a degenerate triangle ((0,1,1)); nondegenerate geometric examples begin at (n=7).

**Theorem 2.4 (primitive consecutive-leg reduction; PROVED, F-D).** In every Pell solution, (n) is odd, so the triangle
$$
(a_n,b_n,d_n)
=n\left(\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n+1}{2},m\right)
$$
is an integer multiple of a primitive Pythagorean triple with **consecutive legs**. Conversely every primitive right triangle with positive consecutive integer legs (u,u+1) belongs to this Pell family with (n=2u+1).

**Proof.** Reducing (n^2-2m^2=-1) modulo 2 makes (n) odd. Put (u=(n-1)/2), (v=(n+1)/2=u+1). Then (u^2+v^2=(n^2+1)/2=m^2), and (\gcd(u,v)=1); hence ((u,v,m)) is primitive. Conversely (u^2+(u+1)^2=m^2) gives ((2u+1)^2-2m^2=-1). QED.

**Corollary 2.5 (even-index obstruction; PROVED, F-D).** If (n) is even, (d_n) is not an integer. This follows from Theorem 2.2 since Pell solutions require odd (n). The stronger statement is specific to this generator, not to every right triangle.

## 3. Prime-centered subfamily

**Definition 3.1 (twin-prime label; F-P).** An even integer (n\ge4) is twin-prime-centered if both (n-1) and (n+1) are prime.

**Theorem 3.2 (disjointness; PROVED, F-P/F-D).** A twin-prime-centered generator index (n) cannot be an integer-diagonal generator index.

**Proof.** Such an (n) is even; Pell indices are odd (Corollary 2.5). QED.

For all twin primes other than (3,5), the center is divisible by 6. The center (n=6) corresponds to twin primes (5,7) and gives (a_6=15,b_6=21,H_6=666).

**Warning.** Neither primality of the *labels* (n\pm1) nor integer closure of the triangular generator supplies a proof about the perfect cuboid problem.

## 4. Ordered-prime-prefix collision

**Definition 4.1 (prime-prefix sums; F-P).** Let (p_1=2,p_2=3,\ldots) be primes in increasing order. Define (S_k=\sum_{i=1}^k p_i^2), (k\ge1).

**Observation 4.2 (EXACT, F-P).**
$$
S_7=2^2+3^2+5^2+7^2+11^2+13^2+17^2
=666=T_{36}=T_{6^2}.
$$
The equality is exact. A generative correspondence between the ordered primes and consecutive triangular legs has **not** been proved.

**Proposition 4.3 (testable Diophantine restatement; PROVED, F-P/F-T).**
$$
S_k=T_{n^2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
8S_k+1=(2n^2+1)^2.
$$
**Proof.** Multiply (S_k=n^2(n^2+1)/2) by 8 and add 1. The reverse follows by subtraction and division. QED.

**Lemma 4.4 (first modular sieve; PROVED, F-P).** For (k\ge2):
$$
S_k\equiv k-1\pmod3,
\\qquad S_k\equiv k+3\pmod8.
$$
**Proof.** The prime 3 contributes 0 modulo 3 and the other (k-1) primes contribute 1. The prime 2 contributes 4 modulo 8 and the other (k-1) odd primes each contribute 1. QED.

**Corollary 4.5 (necessary condition; PROVED, F-P).** If (S_k=T_{n^2}) and (k\ge2), then (k\not\equiv0\pmod3). Indeed, (n^2\equiv0\text{ or }1\pmod3), and therefore (T_{n^2}\equiv0\text{ or }1\pmod3), forcing (k-1\equiv0\text{ or }1\pmod3).

**Computational observation 4.6 (F-C, reproduced 2026-10-17).** An exact-integer scan of all **664,579 primes below 10,000,000** found triangular (S_k) only at
$$
(k,p_k,S_k,t)=(7,17,666,36),\quad(86,443,5022865,3169),
$$
where (S_k=T_t). Only (k=7) had (t=n^2). Finite bounds do not establish uniqueness or significance.

## 5. Explicit frame transitions

1. **F-T → F-G:** interpret ((a_n,b_n)) as perpendicular Euclidean lengths; the hypotenuse is (d_n=\sqrt{H_n}\). No integrality follows from this move.
2. **F-G → F-D:** impose (d_n\in\mathbb Z); Theorem 2.2 gives a nontrivial number-theoretic obstruction.
3. **F-T ↔ F-P:** equality at a numeric value (666) is an observed collision between two separately defined generators, not a proof of shared origin.
4. **F-T/F-D → F-R:** interpreting the exact norm identity as a general principle of relational closure is a *conjectural theoretical translation*.

## 6. Questions to pursue (NOT THEOREMS)

- **H1:** Is there a nontrivial explicit mapping from finite prime-prefix sets to triangular-square configurations preserving something stronger than numeric equality?
- **H2:** Is the 666 collision statistically unexpected under a specified null process modeling prime-prefix growth?
- **H3:** Which alternative polygonal-number generators yield similar norm closure and Pell-classified integer diagonals?
- **H4:** Are there graph, dynamical-system, or invariant formulations where the triangular relation is naturally self-referential, rather than merely an algebraic identity?
- **H5:** Can distinct quadratic fields or angular families combine to impose meaningful arithmetic constraints on multi-face geometric closure? No perfect-cuboid proof is claimed.
